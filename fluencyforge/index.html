FluencyForge

An English conversation tutor that corrects you as you talk, by text or voice, and keeps working when an LLM provider fails or, on the phone, when there is no internet at all.

The web version is a Streamlit app with grammar diffs, vocabulary tracking, flashcards and exports to Anki and PDF. The Flutter app adds a hybrid mode: cloud models when online, a local model running on the device when offline.

Architecture

Two clients,
two ways to stay up.

On the web, availability comes from switching providers. On the phone, it comes from not needing a provider at all.

Engineering decisions

Three calls worth explaining
in an interview.

01 · Availability

Four providers behind one service.

Free tiers rate-limit in the middle of a lesson. The ChatService wraps a fallback chain that tries Groq first, then OpenAI, Gemini and Perplexity, and logs each failed attempt, so a provider outage shows up in the logs instead of in front of the student.

02 · Offline

A model in the pocket.

The Flutter app checks connectivity and, without internet, runs a small model on the device through MediaPipe. The conversation stays on the phone, at the cost of a bigger app and a slower first load.

It only works on real devices, not on emulators, which shaped how it was tested.

03 · Learning

Corrections you can see.

Instead of a corrected sentence buried in the reply, the tutor renders a diff between what the student wrote and the corrected version, and collects new vocabulary that can be exported to Anki or a weekly PDF report.

Limitations

What this project
is not.

  • It is a conversational tutor with provider fallback, not an agent graph or a retrieval system: there is no RAG and LangGraph is not used.
  • Learning outcomes have not been measured; the page makes no claim about how much it improves fluency.
  • The offline model is small and only runs on physical devices; its answers are weaker than the cloud models'.
